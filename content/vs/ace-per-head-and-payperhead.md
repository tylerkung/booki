# Booki vs Ace Per Head: The Cheapest Per Head Is Still Per Head

Ace Per Head advertises [as low as $3 per active player per week](https://aceperhead.com/blog/pph-guide/ace-per-head-pricing/), and only bills a player who had a graded wager that week. That is the lowest advertised rate in this comparison set by some distance. Twenty active players is still about **$260 a month**, or **$3,120 a year**. Booki Pro is **$49.99 a month** for up to 50 members — **$599.88 a year**.

So the honest version of this page is not "Ace is expensive." At $3 they are cheap for what they include. The question is whether you need what they include.

## Who should stay on Ace Per Head

Stay if the full shop is the point. Ace has been in the industry since 1998, and the $3 rate carries a customized sportsbook website, sharp lines updated continuously, a virtual casino, a live dealer casino, horse betting, a custom prop builder, and 24/7 support. Premium live betting with 50–70 in-play options is **$5 extra per week** for players who use it, with the first two weeks free. They advertise up to 20% off for paying in cryptocurrency, and various free-week promotions for new agents.

They are also direct about what they do not do: their own pricing page states plainly that they do not offer $5 or $7 weekly rates. Take that as a signal about positioning rather than a discount to negotiate for.

If your players open the casino, bet horses, or use in-play, that stack is the product and $3 a head is a fair price for not building it. Booki will not replace it.

## Who should switch to Booki

Switch if you organize for roughly 8 to 50 people you already know, you settle outside the software, and nobody has opened the casino, the racebook or the prop builder. At that point the per-head rate is not the problem — buying an operation you do not run is.

Booki is pick-tracking and bankroll management software. **Be The House.** Not a sportsbook, not a pay-per-head service. No payments, no deposits, no payouts, no funds held. Live odds, automated grading for moneylines, spreads, totals and parlays (futures are graded manually), credit and win limits, settlement reconciliation, a tamper-evident ledger, and member invites. Cap: 50 members. No casino, racebook, call center, or branded sportsbook website.

If you needed the house, stay. If you needed a ledger, even $3 a head is buying the wrong thing.
