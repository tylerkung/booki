PPH vs Manual Ledger Systems: Which Is Right for You?

February 24, 2026 · 6 min read · By the Booki Team

If you're running an independent operation, one of the first decisions you'll face is how to manage your ledger. The two most common approaches are pay-per-head (PPH) services and manual tracking — typically spreadsheets, notebooks, or messaging apps. Each has real advantages and real drawbacks, and the right choice depends on where you are in your journey.

In this article, we'll break down both systems honestly, compare their costs and capabilities, and help you figure out which approach fits your situation — or whether there's a better third option.

What Is a PPH Service?

Pay-per-head platforms are hosted services that provide a full turnkey solution for managing picks. You pay a weekly fee for each active member — typically $5 to $15 per head — and get a website, odds feeds, grading, and basic reporting in return.

PPH services have been the industry standard for years. They handle the technical side of things so organizers can focus on managing their members. Most PPH platforms provide:

  • Pre-built member portals where members can browse lines and submit picks
  • Automated odds feeds pulled from major sportsbooks
  • Automatic grading for most standard markets
  • Weekly settlement reports summarizing what each member owes or is owed

The appeal is clear: you get a working system out of the box without building anything yourself.

What Does Manual Ledger Tracking Look Like?

On the other end of the spectrum, many organizers — especially those just starting out — track everything by hand. A simple spreadsheet with columns for date, member, event, pick, odds, stake, result, and balance can work surprisingly well for small operations.

Manual tracking means you're responsible for everything: recording picks, looking up results, calculating payouts, and communicating balances. The tools are simple — Google Sheets, Excel, or even a notebook — but the process is entirely on you.

For organizers with fewer than five members, this approach is often sufficient. The volume is low enough that grading a dozen picks per week takes minutes, not hours.

Cost Comparison

Cost is often the deciding factor, and the math is straightforward:

  • PPH services: $5–$15 per active member per week. With 20 members, that's $100–$300 per week — or $5,200–$15,600 per year. The fee applies whether a member places one pick or fifty.
  • Manual tracking: Free, aside from your time. A Google Sheets document costs nothing, and the formulas aren't complicated. But your time has value, and manual grading at scale can eat hours every week.

For small operations (under 10 members), PPH fees can feel steep relative to the volume of activity. For larger operations, the per-head cost is easier to justify — but it never goes away, and it scales linearly with your member count.

Feature Comparison

Beyond cost, the two approaches differ significantly in what they offer:

  • Grading accuracy: PPH platforms grade automatically using live scores. Manual tracking requires you to look up every result yourself, which introduces human error — especially during busy weekends with dozens of games. As we covered in our guide on common organizer mistakes, grading errors are the leading cause of settlement disputes.
  • Member experience: PPH gives members a portal to browse lines and place picks. Manual tracking usually means picks come in via text message or group chat, which is harder to organize and easier to lose.
  • Scalability: Manual tracking breaks down as you add members. What works for 5 members becomes unsustainable at 20. PPH platforms handle scale better, but each new member adds to your cost.
  • Privacy and control: With PPH, your member data lives on someone else's servers. You're trusting a third party with sensitive information. Manual tracking keeps everything local — but with no backup or audit trail.
  • Customization: PPH platforms offer a standard product. You get what they provide, with limited ability to customize rules, limits, or workflows. Manual tracking is fully flexible — but only because you're doing everything yourself.

When to Use Each Approach

Neither system is universally better. The right choice depends on your situation:

Manual tracking works well when:

  • You have fewer than 5–10 members
  • Pick volume is low (under 30 per week)
  • You're comfortable with spreadsheets and don't mind the weekly time investment
  • You want to keep costs at zero while you're getting started
  • Privacy is a top priority and you don't want data on external servers

PPH services work well when:

  • You have 15+ active members
  • Pick volume is high enough that manual grading takes hours
  • You want members to have a professional portal for browsing lines
  • You're willing to pay per-head fees for the convenience
  • You need automated odds and grading to keep up with volume

The Gap Between the Two

The reality is that many organizers outgrow manual tracking long before they can justify PPH fees. There's a frustrating middle ground where spreadsheets are too slow but $200/month feels excessive for a 15-member operation.

This is exactly the gap that modern PPH alternatives are designed to fill. Tools like Booki offer the automation you need — auto-grading, real-time balances, immutable ledger tracking — without the per-head pricing model that makes traditional PPH services expensive at every scale.

The key differences from both approaches:

  • No per-head fees. Your cost doesn't scale with your member count.
  • Automatic grading and settlement. No manual lookups, no formula errors.
  • Your data stays yours. No third-party portals with your member information.
  • Mobile-first design. Modern interface that members actually want to use, instead of dated PPH websites.
  • Audit trail. Every pick, every grading decision, every settlement is recorded and verifiable — something neither spreadsheets nor most PPH platforms offer.

Making Your Decision

Here's a simple framework for choosing:

  1. If you're just starting out with a handful of members, begin with manual tracking. Learn the rhythms of weekly settlements and grading before investing in any tool.
  2. If manual tracking is eating your weekends and you're making grading mistakes, it's time to automate — but that doesn't mean you need to jump to PPH pricing.
  3. If you're already on a PPH service and the per-head fees are cutting into your margins, evaluate whether a flat-rate alternative gives you the same capabilities at a lower cost.

The best system is the one that matches your current scale, keeps your ledger accurate, and doesn't cost more than it should. Whatever you choose, the important thing is having a system — because running an operation without one is the surest way to lose money and members.
