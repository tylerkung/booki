How to Run Your Own
Independent Sportsbook

Everything you need to know about setting up a ledger, managing members, tracking picks, and running a clean weekly settlement — whether you use spreadsheets or dedicated software.

Your Step-by-Step Sportsbook Setup Guide

01

Set Up Your Ledger

Your ledger is the backbone of your sportsbook. Every pick, every outcome, every settlement flows through it. Start with a spreadsheet template or use dedicated ledger software — just make sure every transaction is recorded from day one. A clean ledger prevents disputes and builds trust with your members.

02

Define Your Rules

Before accepting a single pick, decide on your house rules: what sports and markets you offer, minimum and maximum stakes, credit limits per member, and your settlement schedule. Write them down and share them with everyone. Consistency is what separates a well-run sportsbook from chaos.

03

Recruit & Onboard Members

Start small — 5 to 10 trusted friends is ideal. Give each member a clear starting balance or credit limit. Walk them through how to submit picks, check their balance, and when settlements happen. The easier you make onboarding, the smoother everything runs.

04

Source Your Lines & Odds

You need reliable odds for every game you offer. You can pull lines from major sportsbooks, use an odds API, or set your own. The key is consistency — your members should see the same odds at pick time that you grade against. Real-time odds feeds eliminate discrepancies.

05

Track & Grade Picks

As picks come in, log them immediately: who placed it, the event, the market, the odds, and the stake. When games finish, grade each pick against the final score. Moneyline, spread, and totals each have their own grading logic. Accuracy here is non-negotiable — one grading error can erode member trust.

06

Settle Weekly

Pick a settlement day — Sunday night or Monday morning works for most. Tally each member's net balance for the week, communicate what's owed, and collect or pay out. Keep a record of every settlement. Read our guide on how organizers track weekly settlements for a detailed walkthrough.

Common Challenges You'll Face

Record-Keeping Gets Messy

Spreadsheets break down fast. Missed entries, formula errors, and version confusion lead to disputes. As your group grows, manual tracking becomes a full-time job.

Grading Takes Forever

Checking every final score, calculating spread covers, and grading totals by hand is tedious and error-prone. One wrong grade and your credibility takes a hit.

Scaling Beyond 10 Members

What works for 5 friends falls apart at 15 or 20. More members means more picks, more disputes, and more settlement complexity. You need systems that scale with your group.

Settlement Disputes

When a member disagrees with their balance, you need receipts. Without a clear transaction history, every settlement becomes a negotiation instead of a formality.
